Synchrotron emission
Relativistic electrons in the Galactic magnetic field. Falls steeply with frequency (∝ ν−0.7). Polarized — contaminates B-mode searches at low frequencies. Traced by SO's 27 and 39 GHz bands.
Thermal dust emission
Warm interstellar grains emit a modified blackbody. Rises steeply (∝ ν3.5). Polarized — dominant B-mode foreground at high frequencies. Traced by SO's 225 and 280 GHz bands.
The CMB window
Between ~70–200 GHz both foregrounds are minimal and the CMB dominates. SO's 93 and 145 GHz science bands sit here. Even so, foreground removal is essential for B-modes at the ~10 nK level.
The separation problem: No single frequency band is "clean." Component separation exploits the distinct spectral shapes — synchrotron falls, dust rises, the CMB follows a fixed blackbody, the tSZ effect has a null at 217 GHz — to solve for all components simultaneously across all six bands. This is why 27–280 GHz coverage is essential.
Key instrument note: The SAT (Small Aperture Telescope, 0.5 m) is optimized for large angular scales (ℓ < 300) and B-mode polarization — primarily inflation science. The LAT (Large Aperture Telescope, 6 m, underilluminated to 5.5 m) reaches arcminute resolution for all other science cases. Both share 6 frequency bands: 27, 39, 93, 145, 225, 280 GHz.
