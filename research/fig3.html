SCIENCE WITH THE SIMONS OBSERVATORY

Six science drivers — each requiring a different combination of frequency coverage, angular resolution, and polarization sensitivity

Primordial Universe

Inflation

t < 10⁻³² s after Big Bang
Observable: B-mode polarization at ℓ < 300
Inflation predicts a background of primordial gravitational waves that imprint a faint, distinctive curl-like (B-mode) pattern on CMB polarization at large angular scales. No other known process produces this pattern at these scales.
SO forecast
σ(r) = 0.003
10× improvement over Planck. Planck+BICEP/Keck: r < 0.036
SAT 3 × 0.5-m telescopes, large-scale sky coverage
Early Universe Physics

Light Relics & Neutrinos

t ~ 1 s – 380,000 yrs
Observable: CMB damping tail (TE, TT, EE); lensing
Any particle species in thermal equilibrium in the early Universe leaves a measurable imprint on the CMB damping tail via its contribution to the radiation density Neff. The lensing power spectrum constrains the total neutrino mass.
SO forecast
σ(Neff) ≈ 0.05
σ(Σmν) ≈ 40 meV · Can detect any thermal relic at 95% C.L.
LAT 6-m aperture, arcminute resolution damping tail
Large-Scale Structure

Dark Energy & Clusters

z = 0 – 3 (recent 8 Gyr)
Observable: thermal SZ (tSZ), CMB lensing
Hot gas in galaxy clusters scatters CMB photons, producing a distinctive spectral distortion (tSZ). The CMB lensing power spectrum traces the projected matter distribution. Together they constrain the growth of structure and dark energy.
SO forecast
~16,000 clusters
σ(σ₈) ≈ 0.004 · σ(w₀) competitive with stage-IV BAO surveys
LAT 40% sky coverage, 6 frequency bands for spectral SZ
Cosmic Dawn

Reionization

z ~ 6–12 (0.4–1 Gyr)
Observable: kinematic SZ (kSZ), optical depth τ
During reionization, ionized bubbles of gas around the first stars scatter CMB photons. The bulk velocity of these bubbles produces a kinematic SZ signal. The patchy optical depth constrains when and how reionization proceeded.
SO forecast
Δz / zreion
Constrain duration and morphology · First detection of patchy τ
LAT high-ℓ kSZ via cross-correlation with galaxy surveys
Astrophysics

Galaxy Evolution

z = 0 – 4 (recent 12 Gyr)
Observable: tSZ, kSZ stacking on galaxy samples
The tSZ measures non-thermal pressure and total thermal energy in cluster gas. The kSZ measures bulk gas velocities. Together they probe AGN and supernova feedback — the processes regulating star formation in galaxies.
SO forecast
10⁴ clusters + fields
Non-thermal pressure profiles · Feedback efficiency at z>1
LAT + external galaxy surveys (DESI, LSST, Euclid)

All epochs

>20,000 sources
Multi-frequency SEDs · First SO transient catalog
